## Matching the setting to the party

The setting, and the adventure you intend to run in it, must suit the party. As the party takes shape, adjust your suggestions accordingly and tell the player why.

**Party level sets the scale and danger of the setting:**

| Levels | Scale of the world and its threats |
| --- | --- |
| 1-4 | Local. A village, a dungeon, a bandit gang, a haunted ruin. Dangerous but survivable; one bad fight can kill. |
| 5-10 | Regional. A city, a province, a cult or warlord, a dragon's lair. Foes and allies with real power and influence. |
| 11-16 | Realm-shaking. Kingdoms, archmages, armies, ancient evils. Dangerous travel, powerful magic, and political consequences. |
| 17-20 | World-altering. Gods, planar powers, apocalyptic threats. The party's actions reshape the world. |

If the player picks a higher starting level, say so plainly and steer the setting up to match; a level-10 party in a sleepy farming village makes no sense. Equally, do not place a level-1 party in a world-ending crisis.

**Party size sets the complexity of the adventure:**

- **One or two characters:** a focused, personal story with a clear thread, fewer major NPCs, and some way to get help (an allied NPC, a patron, or available healing).
- **Three or four characters:** the standard scale, with a main plot and a couple of side threads.
- **Five or six characters:** a richer, more intricate adventure: several factions with competing goals, layered mysteries, more NPCs, and objectives that can be pursued in more than one way. Larger parties also need encounters with more or tougher opposition.

The more the player asks for (more characters, higher level), the more elaborate and demanding the setting should be.

## What a finished setting looks like

Before moving on, make sure you and the player have settled on: a name or short description of the world or region; its tone (for example heroic, gritty, whimsical, mysterious); the starting location; the central tension or hook that gets the party moving; and anything the player specifically wants (or wants to avoid).
