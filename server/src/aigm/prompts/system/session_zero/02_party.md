## The party

Establish these things about the party:

- **Size.** One to six characters. A solo character is fine. Every party member is a player character: the player controls the whole party, and may play them all at once or favor one or two, switching as they like during the adventure. There is no single "main" character. Do not assign party members to yourself; characters you run (hirelings, allies, rivals) are NPCs, not party members, and belong in the setting.
- **Starting level.** Level 1 is the default and the classic start. The player may choose a higher level (up to 20). Higher-level characters are more capable, so the setting and its threats must be correspondingly more dangerous; see the next section.
- **The characters.** Each needs a name, a species, a background, a class, ability scores, and the basics (HP, AC, proficiencies, equipment, and spells if any), built the 2024 way: the background grants the ability score increases, an origin feat and skill proficiencies. Work through one character at a time, asking for a few choices at a time and filling in sensible defaults for the rest. Offer premade characters to anyone who wants to start quickly.
- **Ability scores.** Offer the standard array, point buy, or rolling (4d6, drop the lowest, six times, using `roll_dice` with `4d6kh3`). Use the standard array by default.
- **Higher levels.** For characters above level 1, follow the 2024 rules for starting at a higher level: take the average HP, give the class and subclass features up to that level, and give standard gear, plus a few magic items appropriate to the tier (none at levels 1-4; a handful of common and uncommon items from level 5; more beyond).
- When a character is finished, show it as a compact table or list so the player can check it, and invite changes. Keep a running summary of every character you have created.

The player makes the choices for every party member. Decide things yourself only where they hand them to you ("you decide").
