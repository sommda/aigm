Roll a social skill (Persuasion, Deception, Intimidation, Diplomacy, Bluff, haggling, the old editions' reaction rolls) for a party member who is trying to change what a recorded person believes, feels or does. Use it instead of `roll_dice` for these checks. Insight, Sense Motive and other ways of reading a person are not speech: roll those with `roll_dice`.

**The player must say it.** A player who types "I bluff the guard" has declared an intention, not played it. Before you roll, the player must have typed what the character actually says. If they have not, do not call this tool: ask in the story's voice what the character says ("The guard narrows his eyes. What do you tell him?") and end your turn. This tool refuses a call whose `spoken` is not the player's own words from their latest message.

- `character`, `skill`, `target` (a recorded person who is with the party), `modifier` (the character's total for the skill, read from the sheet; for a reaction roll the charisma reaction adjustment) and `dc` (leave out for a reaction roll).
- `spoken`: the words, quoted or trimmed from the player's message (at least four words).
- `quality`: your judgment of the words, on the idea and not the prose. Length, flourish and vocabulary do not count, and a plain, terse line can be the best one. Ask: Is it in character for this character, given the sheet and what they know? Is it plausible to this listener, who can check some things and would believe others? Is it specific, using a name, a detail or a fact the character really has? Does it speak to what this person wants? `weak`: implausible to them, contradicting what they know, out of character, a repeated argument or "I say whatever works". `ordinary`: fine, plausible and nothing more; most lines are this. `strong`: specific, in character and fitted to this person. `brilliant`: strong, and also surprising or costly in a way that fits, such as a risk the character takes or a line only this character could say.
- `engages`: the ids of the target's motives (from your notes) that the words touch, each `aligned` (the words serve it) or `against` (they cut across it). Be honest about it: the server reads the strengths from the record. Use each motive's `moved_by` and `resists` to judge both `quality` and `engages`; a threat to a proud man who resists threats is weak however well put.
- `narration`: shown to the player just before the dice, as for `roll_dice`.

The server turns the quality and the motives into the ruleset's reward (advantage, a bonus, a better reaction roll) and builds the dice itself. A motive that is held absolutely can make the words succeed with no roll, or bar the roll entirely: then the result says so and you narrate it in character. Never tell the player the DC, the reward or the motive.

One check per player message and person; a second try needs new words. Call it before the message that holds your final reply, never in that message and never after it.
