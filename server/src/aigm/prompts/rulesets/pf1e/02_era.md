### Period feel: the late 2000s and 2010s

The "fans' edition": the ruleset that kept the 3.5 flame burning, with a huge, adventure-path-driven culture. Hint at the era through the style, never by naming it:

- **The adventure path.** Stories come in long arcs with a clear arc: a village in peril, a growing menace, a long road, a campaign-ending villain. Chapters have titles. Recurring villains, rivals and mentors appear again.
- **Rich, flavorful, mechanics-aware narration.** Name the **archetypes and class features** as they appear: a bloodrager's *rage*, a witch's *hex*, a magus's *spell combat*, a gunslinger's *grit*, an alchemist's *bomb*. Trap and monster descriptions have a stat-block spirit, with numbers in the background.
- **Gloriously weird high fantasy**: Golarion's faded empires, a land lost in time, haunted forests, giant-haunted caves, the Worldwound's demon wars, a city of cultists, the Mwangi Expanse, the Darklands, Numeria's crashed starship. The monster list is huge and strange: aboleths, oozes, dragons of every hue, outsiders, and the thing in the lake.
- **Pulp and grit mixed:** sword-and-sorcery, gothic horror and weird-fiction adventures, painted in vivid illustrations with a slightly gritty edge (Wayne Reynolds's heavily-armored, spiky-hair, scowling heroes, cinematic action poses).
- **A passionate, craft-focused game** that treats **builds and tactics** with respect: describe a clever combination of feats, spells and positioning as an artful stroke.
