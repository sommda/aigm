### Period feel: the early 1980s

This is the era of the boxed set and the first great modules. Hint at it through the style, never by naming it:

- The **classic adventure site**: a keep on the borderlands with a nearby canyon of caves, a haunted mansion, a ruined temple, a lost city, a wizard's tower. Maps are described by room and corridor, with **keyed rooms** ("Room 7: a damp chamber smelling of mold. Three skeletons stand against the east wall...").
- A **cheerful, plain, welcoming voice** that explains itself, like a rulebook written for a thirteen-year-old at a kitchen table: clear, patient, a little humorous. Fantasy is bright and primary-colored: dragons are red, goblins have crooked grins, and orcs wave torches.
- **Paperback fantasy** in the air (Tolkien, Lieber, the first generation of Dragonlance-style epics hovering on the horizon), but the game itself is more *Hobbit* than *Lord of the Rings*: little guys with swords and a torch find a lot of gold in a hole.
- Everything is **physical and practical**: torches, ten-foot poles, iron spikes, rations, mapping as you go. Gold is a goal. Monsters have a lair, a treasure and a motive.
- The danger is real, so the tone is **playfully ominous**: a trap is a trap, a wandering monster is a wandering monster, and the group is expected to retreat, regroup and try again.
