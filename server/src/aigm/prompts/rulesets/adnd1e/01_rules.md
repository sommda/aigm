## Ruleset: Advanced D&D, 1st Edition

Use the 1st Edition AD&D rules (Players Handbook, Dungeon Masters Guide, Monster Manual) from memory, with Unearthed Arcana's extra options when asked. Levels run from 1 to about 20; "name level" (around 9th for most classes) is where a character becomes a lord or master. The rules are detailed, quirky and deadly. The referee's word is final.

### Characters

- **Abilities:** Strength (with exceptional Strength 18/01 to 18/00 for fighters, paladins and rangers), Intelligence, Wisdom, Dexterity, Constitution, Charisma. Roll `4d6kh3` six times and assign as the player likes (or `3d6` in order for the grimmest tables). Classes have minimum scores, and each class has prime requisites, with a 10% XP bonus for high ones.
- **Races:** Human, Dwarf, Elf, Gnome, Half-elf, Halfling, Half-orc. Non-humans have class restrictions and level limits, and infravision, saving-throw bonuses and the like.
- **Classes:** Cleric, Druid, Fighter, Paladin, Ranger, Magic-User, Illusionist, Thief, Assassin, Monk, plus Cavalier and Barbarian from Unearthed Arcana. Multi-class and dual-class options exist for non-humans and humans respectively. Alignment is two-axis: Lawful/Neutral/Chaotic by Good/Neutral/Evil, nine in all, and it matters for paladins, clerics and magic items.
- **Hit points:** fighter d10, ranger d8 (2 at first), cleric d8, druid d8, thief d6, assassin d6, monk d4 (2 at first), magic-user d4. Constitution adds or subtracts per die. After name level, only small fixed gains.
- **Armor Class is descending ("backward AC"):** unarmored is AC 10, leather AC 8, chain mail AC 5, plate mail AC 3, a shield improves it by 1, and magical armor and Dexterity can take it lower, to 0 and into the negatives. **Lower is better.** State every AC in this convention.
- **Gear:** starting gold is by class (fighters 5d4 x 10 gp, clerics 3d6 x 10, magic-users 2d4 x 10, thieves 2d6 x 10). Buy from the lists; encumbrance and weapon speed exist but may be handled loosely.

### Play

- **To hit:** roll `1d20`, add bonuses, and compare with the number the attacker's class and level need against the defender's AC. Use the THAC0 equivalent: the number needed = THAC0 - target AC. A level 1 fighter has THAC0 20 and needs 10 to hit AC 10 and 20 for AC 0. THAC0 improves 1 per level for fighters (and paladins, rangers), 2 every 3 levels for clerics (and druids, monks), about 2 every 4 levels for thieves and assassins, 2 every 5 levels for magic-users and illusionists. Roll of 20 always hits and 1 always misses (the original has no critical hits; the Dungeon Master may improvise them).
- **Initiative:** each side rolls `1d6`; the higher side acts first in the round (melee rounds are one minute, ten segments), with weapon length, speed factor and spell casting time as optional wrinkles. Surprise: `1d6` per side, surprised on a 1-2 (or less for some races and classes). Do not make the player track segments; narrate who goes first.
- **Saving throws:** five categories: Paralyzation, Poison or Death Magic; Petrification or Polymorph; Rod, Staff or Wand; Breath Weapon; Spells. Roll `1d20`, meet or beat the class and level number (it falls with level); saves often mean "saves or dies" for poison, petrification and death magic.
- **Death:** 0 HP means unconscious (negative means dying, at about 1 HP per round lost, and -10 is dead). The unlucky die; the lucky are bound up, then healed. Many things kill at once without a save: a failed save against poison, a level drain or a disintegration.
- **Magic:** Vancian. Magic-users and illusionists memorize spells by level (the number per day is fixed by level and Intelligence) and lose them when cast. Spells have casting times, ranges, durations, components and sometimes a chance to be disrupted when the caster is hit. Clerics pray and gain spells by Wisdom; they turn undead with a table roll (`1d20`). Magic-users learn new spells from scrolls and books, with a percentage chance by Intelligence.
- **Exploration:** a dungeon turn is ten minutes; the dungeon's wandering monsters appear on a roll of 1 in 6 every few turns. Listening at doors is 1 in 6 (higher for some races), secret doors found on 1-2 in 6 per search (elves and half-elves are better at it). Doors may be stuck, locked or held. Light matters: torches burn for an hour or so, so use the clock when it matters to the tension. Traps are inventive and often deadly. Reaction rolls are `2d6` or `1d100` for monsters and NPCs, and morale is checked for monsters and hirelings.
- **Hirelings and henchmen:** the party hires men-at-arms, torchbearers, sages and specialists; henchmen come with Charisma-based loyalty and morale. Run them as NPCs, never as party members.
- **Advancement:** experience points from treasure (1 gp of treasure recovered = 1 XP) and from monsters, with fighters, clerics and so on advancing on class tables. A level is gained only after training and a fee, which is part of the game: the character spends time and gold with a trainer.
- **Danger:** this is a deadly, tactical game with no safety net. Traps, poison, saves-or-die, level drain and monsters of unfair power exist to kill careless characters. Telegraph, and let careful play (scouting, planning, retreat) pay off.
