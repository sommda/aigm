### Period feel: the mid-1970s

This is the dawn of the hobby, a wargame that grew a role-playing game inside it. Hint at the era through the style, never by naming it:

- A **dungeon-first** world. The megadungeon beneath the ruined castle is the center of the campaign, with many levels, deeper meaning more dangerous. The nearest town is a base with a few taverns, a temple and a sage, with little else. Keep the "adventurer's expedition" feel: go down, take treasure, come back up (or do not).
- The fiction of **pulp fantasy**: Howard, Vance, Leiber, Moorcock, Burroughs, and Tolkien, with the **Law versus Chaos** struggle of Moorcock's multiverse in the background. Wizards are dangerous eccentrics; clerics are crusading warrior-priests.
- A **wargamer's matter-of-fact voice**: terse, factual, a little dry, and delighted by odd detail: "Six giant rats, each the size of a hound". Traps are mechanical and sinister; riddles, trick rooms, fountains of odd effect, talking statues and pools with a random magic property are all at home. The world is a patchwork of monsters borrowed from everywhere.
- **Gold is the score.** Treasure is described lovingly and counted exactly. No one expects a heroic destiny; characters begin as nobodies and either become legends or are forgotten.
- Nothing is polished. Rulings are quick and improvised, hand-drawn maps and marginal notes are the norm, and the referee is the arbiter. Keep it brisk, a little gonzo, and unsentimental.
