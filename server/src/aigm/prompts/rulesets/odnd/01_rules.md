## Ruleset: Original D&D (1974)

Use the original "white box" rules (Men & Magic, Monsters & Treasure, Underworld & Wilderness Adventures) with the Greyhawk-era supplements' common options. Levels run 1-20 or so, but play is mostly at the low and middle levels. The rules are loose and brief: the referee fills the gaps by judgment, and that is part of the game.

### Characters

- **Abilities:** Strength, Intelligence, Wisdom, Constitution, Dexterity, Charisma. Roll 3d6 for each, in order (`3d6`). A high prime requisite for the class adds a modest XP bonus (about 5-10%). A character may trade 2 points of a non-prime score for 1 in the prime requisite. Modifiers are small: only extremes matter.
- **Races:** Human, Dwarf, Elf, Hobbit (halfling). Demihumans have level limits. Elves may switch between fighting-man and magic-user between adventures.
- **Classes:** Fighting-Man (prime requisite Strength), Magic-User (Intelligence), Cleric (Wisdom; clerics gain spells at 2nd level). With the supplements: Thief, Paladin. Alignment is Law, Neutrality or Chaos.
- **Hit points:** one die per level (d6 for everyone in the original books; if the supplement's variable dice are used, d8 fighting-men, d6 clerics, d4 magic-users). A new character starts with one die.
- **Armor Class is descending:** 9 is unarmored, leather 7, chain 5, plate 3, a shield improves it by 1. Lower is better, and it can go below zero.
- **Gear:** roll 3d6 x 10 gold pieces and buy gear. Money matters.

### Play

- **To hit:** roll `1d20` and compare with the attacker's target number for the defender's AC (the Alternative Combat System). Use the equivalent: a first-level fighting-man hits AC 9 on 10 or better, and each point of lower AC needs 1 more. Fighters improve their chance steadily; clerics slower; magic-users slowest. A natural 1 misses and a natural 20 hits. Damage is typically 1d6 for any weapon in the original rules, 1d6 plus or minus for variable-damage options.
- **Initiative:** each side rolls `1d6`; the higher goes first. Surprise: `1d6`, surprised on a 1-2.
- **Saving throws:** five categories (Death Ray or Poison, Wands, Paralysis or Petrification, Dragon Breath, Staves and Spells). Roll `1d20` and meet or beat the number, which falls with level.
- **Death:** 0 HP is death. There is no dying phase. Raising the dead is expensive and rare.
- **Magic:** magic-users and clerics memorize spells each day by level and forget them when cast; spells are few and powerful. Magic-users keep spell books.
- **Exploration:** a dungeon turn is ten minutes; track light, doors, listening and wandering monsters (about a 1 in 6 chance every few turns). Hirelings and henchmen are normal. Reaction rolls for monsters and NPCs use `2d6`. Morale checks use `2d6`.
- **Advancement:** experience points come mostly from treasure (1 gold piece of treasure brought back = 1 XP) and from slaying monsters. Level up when the total is reached; track it.
- **Danger:** the game is lethal and tactical. Traps, poison and monsters kill low-level characters quickly, and clever play (scouting, hiring help, using the environment) is how they survive.
